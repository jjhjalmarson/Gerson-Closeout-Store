{# What the buyer would sell it for, from what they offer (JJ, 2026-09-03). The buyer sets two numbers once — the freight factor they carry and the margin they need — and every line then answers the only question they actually have: "if I buy it at my price, what do I put on the shelf?" landed = offer / (1 - freight factor) your retail = landed / (1 - target margin) MSRP sits beside it as the anchor, so the gap between the two is the story: what it sold for, and what they could sell it for. It also runs BACKWARDS (2026-09-04), which is the half that matters on a sheet of 500 blank boxes: given the same two numbers, the price they would have to pay to hit their margin selling at MSRP is suggested = MSRP x (1 - target margin) x (1 - freight factor) -- the exact inverse of the line above. It appears under each offer box as a suggestion they can take with one click, and "Fill this page" takes all of the empty ones at once. It is still their own arithmetic on numbers already on the page: no cost, no floor and no ladder step is disclosed, and neither of their two numbers is ever sent to Gerson. Filling a price does NOT create an offer line -- only a quantity does that -- so nothing is committed by looking. Every row carries [data-msrp] and an input.offer (or [data-price], where the price is fixed, as on a counter); the answer goes into td.retail, and td.retail-total, if present, gets the retail value of the whole buy. All of it is their arithmetic, in their browser, kept in their own storage. Neither number is ever sent to Gerson. #}
