{# What the buyer would sell it for, from what they offer (JJ, 2026-09-03). The buyer sets two numbers once — the freight factor they carry and the margin they need — and every line then answers the only question they actually have: "if I buy it at my price, what do I put on the shelf?" landed = offer / (1 - freight factor) your retail = landed / (1 - target margin) MSRP sits beside it as the anchor, so the gap between the two is the story: what it sold for, and what they could sell it for. It also runs BACKWARDS (2026-09-04), which is the half that matters on a sheet of 500 blank boxes: given the same two numbers, the price they would have to pay to hit their margin selling at MSRP is suggested = MSRP x (1 - target margin) x (1 - freight factor) -- the exact inverse of the line above. It appears under each offer box as a suggestion they can take with one click, and "Fill this page" takes all of the empty ones at once. It is still their own arithmetic on numbers already on the page: no cost, no floor and no ladder step is disclosed, and neither of their two numbers is ever sent to Gerson. CAPPED (2026-09-08). That sum alone is circular on this sheet: MSRP is not a NetSuite field, it is msrp_price(wholesale) = wholesale / (1 - 0.60), so suggested = 2.5 x wholesale x (1 - margin) x (1 - freight) is a fixed multiple of wholesale on EVERY line, carrying nothing about the item. At the settings a buyer actually had on 2026-09-08 (50% margin, 25% freight) that multiple is 0.9375 -- the sheet was telling a closeout buyer to offer 94% of wholesale ($20.62 against $21.95), which reads as broken. The suggestion is therefore capped at ``suggest_max_disc`` off wholesale so it is always closeout-shaped. AND AT OUR OWN PUBLISHED PRICE (JJ, 2026-09-08). The flat cap was still one number for 8,400 items, and on the written-down ones it anchored miles above reality: "use $111.22" on a SKU published at $69.51 that we would have taken $20 for. So where the page provides ``data-suggest-max`` -- the lower of the flat cap and the ladder step the website already charges (``suggest_cap`` in app.py) -- that governs instead. It is a ceiling on the buyer's arithmetic, never rendered as a price, and it is not the floor: what we would TAKE stays in AOI and crosses nothing. Filling a price does NOT create an offer line -- only a quantity does that -- so nothing is committed by looking. Every row carries [data-msrp] and an input.offer (or [data-price], where the price is fixed, as on a counter); the answer goes into td.retail, and td.retail-total, if present, gets the retail value of the whole buy. All of it is their arithmetic, in their browser, kept in their own storage. Neither number is ever sent to Gerson. #}
